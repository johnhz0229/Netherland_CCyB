# Storyline (v4, 2026-10-01): "The Worst Possible Moment?"

**Title:** *Refilling the Buffer in a Storm: The Netherlands' CCyB, 0% → 2% (2022–2024)*

## The story in five sentences
1. **Prologue:** In March 2020, when COVID hit, the Netherlands had no countercyclical buffer to release. DNB had to improvise, and it promised to rebuild that capital as a 2% CCyB once things were normal.
2. **The question:** In 2022–23 DNB kept the promise in what looked like the worst possible moment: war, energy shock, 11.6% inflation, an ECB hiking cycle, a housing market turning, and a Basel rulebook that said 0%. Wasn't this exactly the procyclical tightening the CCyB exists to prevent?
3. **Act 1, the environment:** The setting held arguments on both sides. The macro outlook darkened, but the economy had recovered, credit was cold, and banks were strong and profitable.
4. **Act 2, criteria and risks:** DNB was not fighting a credit boom. It applied its new framework, a 2% "normal-times" buffer calibrated on past losses, to insure against shocks no indicator predicts. It handed the one hot spot, housing, to other tools, and cut structural buffers in return.
5. **Act 3, implications:** The twist is that the rate shock *paid* for the buffer. Profitable banks absorbed it at low cost, borrowers saw no sign of contraction, monetary and macroprudential policy complemented each other, and the Netherlands now holds €6.7bn it can release overnight. **Epilogue:** next time, DNB will have something to release.

**Thesis.** *Normalisation, not tightening.* Measured against the pre-COVID baseline, DNB re-composed bank capital (less structural, more releasable) and did it at the moment it was cheapest. Measured against 2021 it was a modest increase, and we say so.

## Slide 1 · One-pager (stand-alone)
**Title:** *The Netherlands filled its CCyB to 2% when the rulebook said 0%, refilling a buffer, not fighting a boom.*

- **Decision:**
  - 0% → 1% (announced May 2022) → 2% (announced May 2023, binding 31 May 2024).
  - Combined with lower O-SII buffers from the same date.
- **Q1 Environment:**
  - The economy had recovered from COVID, then war, energy and inflation hit (HICP 11.6% in 2022) and the ECB raised rates from July 2022.
  - House prices first boomed (+19% y/y), then fell.
  - Credit was cold: the Basel gap was −33pp and −47pp at the decisions, so the buffer guide said 0%.
  - Banks were strong (CET1 17.7%).
- **Q2 Criteria and risks:**
  - DNB's 2022 framework sets 2% as the "standard risk environment" level, calibrated on past crisis losses (€12bn) rather than on credit growth.
  - The target is shocks no indicator predicts (war, energy, rapid tightening, debt sustainability).
  - Housing risks are left to borrower-based tools and the risk-weight floor.
  - It keeps the March-2020 promise to restore releasable capital.
- **Q3 Implications:**
  - **Cheap:** at most about one fifth of large banks' CET1 headroom.
  - **Largely a swap:** structural buffers were cut in 2020 and 2024.
  - **No sign of contraction** in credit or its price.
  - Complements ECB tightening rather than doubling it.
  - Reciprocity means foreign banks hold it too.
  - **€6.7bn** can now be released overnight.
- **Trade-off:** it relies on discretion and credibility, and the release mechanism is untested.

## PROLOGUE (Zheng (A))

### Slide 2 · March 2020: an empty buffer
**Title:** *When COVID hit, neighbours released their buffers; the Netherlands had nothing to release.*
- **Visual:** a "COVID fortnight" strip from the ESRB table ✔.
  - CCyB releases: Denmark 12 Mar, Norway (to 1%) 13 Mar, Sweden 16 Mar, Lithuania and Germany 31 Mar, France and Ireland 1 Apr.
  - NL: 0%.
- **Story:**
  - DNB improvised on 17 Mar 2020. It cut the systemic buffers of ING, Rabobank and ABN AMRO (3% → 2.5/2/1.5%) and postponed the mortgage risk-weight floor: €8bn freed, supporting up to €200bn of lending ✔.
  - Then it made a promise (quote on slide) ✔: *"Once the situation is back to normal, DNB will compensate the systemic buffers reduction by gradually increasing the countercyclical capital buffer to 2%."*
- **Last line:** "Why does it matter which buffer you hold? Because not all capital is usable in a crisis."

### Slide 3 · Primer: the one buffer built to be released
**Title:** *The CCyB is the only layer of bank capital designed to be switched off in a crisis.*
- **Visual (new figure):** the capital stack.
  - Layers: P1 4.5% → P2R → CCoB 2.5% → O-SII/SRB → CCyB.
  - The MDA line is marked, and the CCyB is highlighted as releasable.
- **Design:**
  - 0–2.5% of RWA on domestic exposures.
  - Increases take effect after 12 months; releases take effect immediately.
  - EU reciprocity.
- **Why release matters:**
  - Banks near buffer thresholds cut lending in COVID even when *allowed* to use buffers, because of stigma and MDA restrictions (Couaillier et al. 2022) ✔.
  - Only an explicitly released buffer is truly usable.
- **Last line:** "Two years later, DNB filled exactly that layer, and the timing looks very strange."

### Slide 4 · The question
**Title:** *DNB raised capital requirements in the middle of a storm, when its own rulebook said zero. Wrong moment?*
- **Visual:** the storm on one side, the decision on the other.
  - Storm: war in Ukraine · energy shock · 11.6% inflation · ECB hikes from Jul-2022 · house prices turning · Basel gap −33/−47pp → 0%.
  - Decision: CCyB 0 → 1 → 2%.
- **The worry:**
  - Raising capital in a downturn is the *procyclical* mistake the CCyB was invented to avoid.
  - Was DNB tightening into a slowdown? Or fighting a hidden boom?
- **Roadmap:** three acts, which are the assignment's three questions:
  - *the setting* (environment)
  - *the decision* (criteria and risks)
  - *the consequences* (implications)

## ACT 1 · THE SETTING, Q1 Environment (Zheng (A))

### Slide 5 · Timeline
**Title:** *The path to 2% was announced in 2020 and walked step by step.*
- **Visual (new figure):** a timeline.
  - 17 Mar 2020: buffer cut + promise
  - 29 Dec 2020: SRB → O-SII
  - 1 Jan 2022: mortgage risk-weight floor
  - Feb 2022: new CCyB framework
  - 25 May 2022: 1% announced
  - 31 May 2023: 2% + O-SII cuts announced
  - 31 May 2024: both binding
  - 2024–Sep 2026: held at 2%
  - 30 Nov 2026: floor expires
- **Overlay:** ECB hike dates and the inflation peak, to show the decisions sat inside the tightening cycle ✔.
- **Last line:** "What did the economy look like at those two dates?"

### Slide 6 · Macro: from recovery to storm
**Title:** *The economy had fully recovered when war, energy and inflation darkened the outlook.*
- **Recovery:** GDP back above its pre-COVID level in 2021Q3; end-2021 almost 3% above end-2019 ✔.
- **Then the storm (DNB FSR 2022 ✔):**
  - "the economic outlook has worsened due to the war in Ukraine and high inflation"
  - energy prices at record levels; supply disruptions
- **Inflation and rates:**
  - HICP 11.6% in 2022 ✔.
  - The ECB raised rates by 50bp on 21 Jul 2022, its first hike ✔.
- **Last line:** "In finance, the storm hit two places: housing and interest rates."

### Slide 7 · Financial: credit cold, housing hot, then cooling
**Title:** *Credit was cold; housing was the one hot spot, and it turned.*
- **Visual:** Fig. 2 panels for house prices, debt ratios and the debt-service ratio.
- **Credit:**
  - Basel gap at its 2005–19 minimum.
  - Household debt-service ratio 14.6%, the lowest since 2005 ✔.
  - Household debt/GDP falling: 110 → 107 → 100% ✔.
- **Housing:**
  - Nominal prices +19% y/y in 2022Q1; real prices 16% above the 2007 peak ✔.
  - DNB itself (FSR 2022 ✔): *"the overheated housing market calls for further measures"*.
  - Then real prices fell about 9% (2022Q2–2023Q2) ✔ as mortgage rates rose.
- **Structural:** household debt 107% of GDP, almost twice the euro area's 57.7% ✔.
- **Last line:** "Who would carry the losses if this went wrong? The banks."

### Slide 8 · Banks: strong, and getting stronger from higher rates
**Title:** *Banks entered the storm well capitalised, and rising rates were lifting their profits.*
- **Capital:** CET1 17.7% at end-2021 (EU 15.7%) and 16.3% at end-2022 ✔.
- **Stress tests:** banks stay above the minimum (16.6 → 13.3% in FSR 2022; four major banks −3.8pp to 11.5% in FSR 2023) ✔.
- **Profits:**
  - Net interest income +6.7% in 2022, 67.7% of income ✔.
  - DNB (FSR 2022): higher rates "may ultimately have a positive impact on the profitability of financial institutions" ✔.
- **Setting summary on slide:** *against* raising: war, inflation, hikes, housing turning. *For* raising: recovered economy, cold credit, strong and profitable banks.
- **Hand-over line:** "That was the setting. Now: what did DNB actually look at, and what was it insuring against?"

## ACT 2 · THE DECISION, Q2 Criteria & risks (Diego (B))

### Slide 9 · Why not follow the Basel rule?
**Title:** *The Basel rule said 0%, but on Dutch data it had missed the last crisis and raised false alarms.*
- **The rule:** gap < 2pp → 0%, > 10pp → 2.5% (Drehmann et al. 2010 ✔).
- **Visual:** Fig. 1, our replication matching the BIS gap within 0.00005pp over 1971–2026 ✔.
  - **Missed:** −13pp in 2007Q4.
  - **False alarm:** +17pp in 2012, in a bust (Repullo & Saurina 2011 ✔).
  - **Rewritten:** 2016Q1 −0.6pp in real time vs +26.9pp ex post (Edge & Meisenzahl 2011 ✔).
- **At the decisions:** −33pp and −47pp, so 0%.
- **Footnote:** alternative filters are noisy around COVID (two-sided HP +4.2pp in 2021Q4), which is one more reason not to trigger on any of them.
- **Last line:** "So DNB used a different compass."

### Slide 10 · The compass: DNB's 2022 framework
**Title:** *DNB's framework sets 2% as the normal-times level, calibrated on past crisis losses, not on credit growth.*
- **Four phases ✔:** recovery (released) → **normality (2%)** → increased risk (>2%) → materialisation (release). Build-up of "1% per year … the neutral level of 2% after two years".
- **Calibration ✔:** peak accumulated losses of €12bn (2007–16) → 2% ≈ €6bn releasable → up to €150bn of extra lending headroom. "Also taking into account the impact of the buffer reduction in March 2020."
- **Criteria:** a dashboard of macro, financial-sector, household and corporate, and market indicators. "No mechanical link … guided discretion" ✔.
- **Not alone:**
  - BCBS (2024) lists 17 jurisdictions with a positive neutral rate ✔. NL, Sweden, UK and Poland target 2% ✔.
  - ECB loss-based calibration gives 1.1–1.8% (De Nora et al. 2025) ✔.
- **Last line:** "What did the compass read at the two decisions?"

### Slide 11 · What DNB said
**Title:** *DNB justified the steps by recovery, uncertainty and its 2020 promise, not by credit growth.*
- **2022 (FSR 2022 ✔):**
  - *"When the systemic risk buffers … were lowered in March 2020, we also announced our intention to restore the buffers by raising the CCyB."*
  - *"The risk profile is currently dominated by uncertainty caused by the war in Ukraine, but … the robust economic recovery … provides grounds for a gradual build-up."*
  - Risks "normal to elevated" ✔.
- **2023 (press release ✔):**
  - "the credit-to-GDP gap shows no signs of excessive credit growth"
  - real-estate prices falling
  - institutional investors' risk appetite and concerns about debt sustainability
  - banks "robust, partly due to rising interest rates"
- **Last line:** "So which risks was the buffer meant to cover?"

### Slide 12 · Risks addressed: one tool per risk
**Title:** *The CCyB insures against shocks nobody can forecast; the known hot spots got their own tools.*
- **Visual:** a two-column risk → tool map.

| Financial-stability risk (DNB FSR 2022/23 ✔) | Tool |
|---|---|
| War, energy and supply shocks; rapid tightening of financial conditions and market corrections; debt sustainability of governments, firms and households; banking turmoil (SVB/Credit Suisse, 2023) | **CCyB** (releasable, any shock) |
| "Overheated" housing; high household mortgage debt; interest-only loans | LTV/LTI limits, tax reform, **mortgage risk-weight floor** (2022–Nov 2026) |
| Size and concentration of large banks (sector 400% → 280% of GDP) | **O-SII buffer** |

- **Principle:** Tinbergen (1952) ✔, one instrument per target.
- **Last line:** "If the CCyB wasn't aimed at housing or credit, what was DNB really doing? Let's test the two readings."

### Slide 13 · The verdict (analytical core, the clearest slide)
**Title:** *Every prediction fits normalisation; none fits overheating.*

| If DNB were… | Normalising | Fighting overheating | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Responding to credit indicators | no link | rises with them | gap, debt-service ratio and household debt ratio falling; CCyB up ✔ |
| Path | +1pp a year, stop at 2% | follows risk | 1% → 2% → held ✔ |
| 2023: house prices falling, rates jumping | raise anyway | pause | raised ✔ |
| 2024–26: house prices rising again (+10% y/y, Mar-2026) | stay at 2% | go above 2% (phase 3) | held at 2% ✔ |
| Other buffers | cut structural buffers to compensate | keep them | cut in 2020 and 2024 ✔ |

- **Spoken:** "Five predictions, five matches. And the last row is the key to the whole story."

### Slide 14 · A swap, not a squeeze
**Title:** *DNB moved capital from structural to releasable buffers, leaving the total roughly unchanged.*
- **Visual (new figure):** stacked buffer requirement for a large bank in 2019 → 2020 → 2024, showing structural buffers shrinking and the CCyB growing.
- **Numbers:**
  - 2020: SRB −0.5 to −1.5pp.
  - 2024: O-SII −0.25 to −0.75pp, ≈ €2.7bn (own calculation).
  - CCyB +€6.7bn.
  - Net 2023 step ≈ +€0.7bn.
- **DNB's own words ✔:**
  - 2020: "more or less capital-neutral for the three large banks"
  - 2023: "limited increase in the net capital requirements" (Knot, 7 Jun 2023)
  - FSR 2023: "slightly increases"
- **Not unusual:** the UK offset its 2% via lower Pillar 2A and resolution requirements ✔.
- **Last line:** "So it was not a squeeze. But even a swap has costs, especially in a storm. Back to our question: was it the wrong moment?"

## ACT 3 · THE CONSEQUENCES, Q3 Implications

### Slide 15 · The twist: the rate shock paid for it (Diego (B))
**Title:** *The storm made it the cheapest moment: higher rates gave banks the profits to build the buffer.*
- **For banks:**
  - The full 2% absorbs ≈8% (2021) to ≈19% (2022) of the four large banks' CET1 headroom, as upper bounds ✔.
  - **Visual:** headroom by bank.
- **Why cheap:** net interest income +6.7% in 2022 ✔. Profitable banks build buffers from retained earnings instead of cutting loans.
- **ECB evidence ✔:**
  - Macroprudential Bulletin 24 (2024): "a gradual build-up of the buffer and favourable banking sector conditions (e.g. high profitability) limit these economic costs".
  - Macroprudential Bulletin 31 (2025), Box 2: activating buffers during the 2022–23 tightening "can mitigate implementation costs".
- **Answer to the slide-4 question:** "Not the wrong moment. Arguably the right one."
- **Last line:** "Cheap for banks, but did borrowers pay?"

### Slide 16 · Borrowers: no sign of contraction (Diego (B))
**Title:** *Credit kept flowing, and its price moved with the rest of the euro area.*
- **Visual:** Fig. 3, the event study (NL vs clean controls AT, FI, IT, MT, LU, with a placebo band).
- **Rates:** Dutch lending rates stayed inside the placebo range at all three dates.
- **Volumes:**
  - Household credit grew faster than peers.
  - Bank credit 2022–26: NL +16.6% vs euro area +7.9%, but NL lagged in 2019–22 (+4.3% vs +11.3%).
- **DNB (17 Sep 2026 ✔):** "no signs of constraints in bank credit supply".
- **Caveat on slide:** one treated country, contaminated controls, housing rebound. This is "no sign of contraction", not a causal effect.
- **Hand-over line:** "So the decision was cheap and credit kept flowing. But a CCyB reaches far beyond Dutch banks and borrowers."

### Slide 17 · Monetary and macroprudential policy: complements, not a double squeeze (Zheng (A))
**Title:** *Building the buffer while the ECB hiked did not double the squeeze. It let monetary policy focus on inflation.*
- **The concern:** two tightenings at once, ECB rates plus capital requirements.
- **What happened:** lending rates did not diverge from peers (slide 16), and banks' profits rose with rates (slide 15).
- **ECB (2025) ✔:** early CCyB activation "helps monetary policy focus on its primary objective of price stability, thereby largely eliminating the potential for conflict between price stability and financial stability."
- **DNB (FSR 2022) ✔:** "a sudden tightening of financial conditions could also have a negative impact on financial stability". The releasable buffer is the insurance against that.
- **Euro-area angle:** one monetary policy, but national macroprudential policy. The CCyB is the Dutch-specific lever.
- **Last line:** "And that lever also reaches across borders."

### Slide 18 · Across borders
**Title:** *Through reciprocity, every bank lending in the Netherlands holds the 2%, and Europe is moving the same way.*
- **Reciprocity:**
  - Foreign banks with Dutch exposures must hold the Dutch 2% ✔ (DNB 2023).
  - This limits leakage to foreign lenders (Aiyar, Calomiris & Wieladek 2014 ✔); non-bank leakage remains.
- **Convergence (ESRB ✔):**
  - France 1% (Jan 2024), Germany 0.75% (Feb 2023), Belgium 1% (Oct 2024), Ireland 1.5% (Jun 2024)
  - Portugal 0.75% (Jan 2026), Spain 1% (Oct 2026)
- **A trend in the banking union:** five banking-union countries had positive-neutral frameworks by April 2023 (LT, EE, IE, CY, NL), and the Netherlands set the highest rate at 2% (ECB Macroprudential Bulletin 21) ✔.
- **Last line:** "What did the Netherlands gain from all this?"

### Slide 19 · Resilience gained
**Title:** *Next time, DNB can release €6.7bn overnight.*
- **The buffer:**
  - Releasable-by-design capital went from €0 to ≈€6.7bn, about half of the €12bn post-GFC peak losses.
  - Released, it could support up to €150bn of lending ✔.
- **State-dependence (Lang & Menno 2023 ✔):** a 1pp rise costs about 0.1% of lending in normal times, while a 1pp release can add up to about 10% in bad times.
- **Growing role:** the mortgage risk-weight floor expires on 30 Nov 2026. DNB: this "underpins the importance of … the current CCyB of 2%" ✔.
- **Last line:** "That sounds like a clean win. It isn't entirely."

### Slide 20 · Critiques and open questions
**Title:** *The price of discretion is credibility, and the buffer has never been tested.*
- **Credibility:** without a rule, markets must trust DNB's judgement and communication (Kydland & Prescott 1977 ✔).
- **Calibration:** why 2%? The ECB loss-based range is 1.1–1.8%, and 2% was the highest neutral rate among banking-union frameworks in 2023 (ECB Macroprudential Bulletin 21) ✔.
- **Untested:** will banks lend the freed capital, or hoard it?
- **Baseline honesty:** relative to 2021, it was a modest increase (+€3.3bn before the O-SII offset).
- **Excess-profit levies** in 2022–23 (ECB 2025 notes NL among the adopters ✔) partly used the same profits that fund the buffer.

## EPILOGUE (Zheng (A))

### Slide 21 · Back to March 2020
**Title:** *A promise kept: the buffer was refilled in the storm, at the moment it was cheapest.*
- **Three answers to the assignment:**
  1. **Environment:** a recovered economy hit by war, inflation and rate hikes; cold credit; housing hot then cooling; strong, profitable banks.
  2. **Criteria and risks:** a 2% normal-times level calibrated on past losses, aimed at unforeseeable shocks, keeping the 2020 promise. Housing went to other tools, and structural buffers were cut in return.
  3. **Implications:** cheap, largely a swap, no sign of contraction, complementary to monetary policy, reciprocated abroad, and €6.7bn releasable. The cost is reliance on discretion.
- **Closing line:** *"In March 2020, the Netherlands had nothing to release. Next time, it will. The best time to fill a buffer is when nobody thinks you need it, even in a storm."*

## Claims we deliberately do NOT make
- No causal claim on lending: only "no sign of contraction".
- Not "every gap measure says 0%": only the official Basel gap does at both decisions.
- Not "zero capital released in 2020": €8bn was freed by improvisation, but none of it was releasable by design.
- Not "no tightening at all": relative to 2021 there was a modest net increase.
- Not "DNB saw no overheating": DNB called housing "overheated" in 2022, but did not use the CCyB for it.
- Nothing that rests only on search extracts (e.g. ING's 47bp CCyB).
